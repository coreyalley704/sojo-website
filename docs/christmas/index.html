Two miles down Church, Buffalo and Union — and somewhere in the middle of it, a float our people build on cold Tuesday nights in a parking lot. Look for us. We'll be the ones waving at your kids.

Kevin McCallister spends that whole movie left behind at Christmas while his family tears across the country trying to get back to him. November 28 · Saturday evening
Carols, candles

& coffee.

An old tradition, ninety minutes up the road.

Since the 1700s, Moravians in North Carolina have gathered in December for something simple: candles, a sweet roll, coffee, and singing. No performance. No offering. Nobody asking you for anything.

Just people in a dark room holding small lights, eating warm bread, and singing songs older than this country about a baby who changed everything.

We want to give Concord that — not a program we invented last year, but something ancient, practiced, and true, handed to us by people who did it first.

  • Gibson Mill · 325 McGill Ave NW, Suite 148
  • Warm bread and hot coffee, free
  • A candle for every person who walks in
  • Carols everybody already knows
  • About thirty minutes, start to finish
  • No offering. No sign-up. No catch.

December 11 & 13 · and Christmas Eve
Candles, carols,

& the oldest story.

We turn the lights off, hand everybody a candle, and tell it straight.

Carolers in the atrium when you walk in. A dark room that slowly fills with light, one candle at a time, until you can see every face in it. The Christmas story told the way it actually happened — to a teenager nobody believed, in a town nobody respected, on a night nobody was watching.

Bring your parents. Bring the kid who won't sit still. Bring the friend who hasn't been inside a church since they were twelve. Nobody here is keeping score.

Thursday, December 24 · 6:00 PM
Christmas Eve

at the mill.

One service. Candles, carols, and you're home before the night is over.

This is the one people bring family to. The in-laws who are in town. The brother who only comes at Christmas. The neighbor who has been meaning to for three years. Nobody here is counting how long it's been.

We start at six, hand everybody a candle, sing the songs you already know, and tell the story the way it actually happened — and you're back in your own living room with the evening still ahead of you.

  • One service, 6:00 PM, about an hour
  • A candle for every person who walks in
  • Kids stay with you — this one is for the whole family
  • Come as you are. Nobody dresses up for this.

Where we are.

Gibson Mill is big, and the first time you pull in it can feel like you're in the wrong place. You're not. Here's exactly where to turn, where to park, and which door is ours.

  • A LotFirst-time guests, parents with young kids, and anyone with mobility needs. First on your left as you come in.
  • B LotThe grass field. Everybody else.
  • C LotOverflow, just past the B Lot.
  • Volunteer LotOur team parks on the far side so the close spots stay open for guests.
  1. Enter from McGill Ave. If your map app gets confused, search "Gibson Mill Concord" and head for the main entrance.
  2. Look for the vests. Our parking team is outside waving people in. Tell them it's your first time.
  3. Our door is beside the City Club entrance, under the large SOJO Church sign mounted at the top of the building.
  4. Walk the hallway to the end. Guest Services is waiting there, and they'll take it from there.
  5. Kids check in on the way. Nursery is off the café on your left; first-time families check in at the guest station outside the SOJO Kids entrance.
Heads up

On Market day (Nov 28) and Festival weekend (Dec 5–6) the whole Mill is packed and the lots fill early. Give yourself an extra fifteen minutes, or park in the B Lot and walk.
