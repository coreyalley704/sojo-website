Grow

Not the fruit.
The water

You do not fix fruit by working on fruit. You change what the roots drink.

GrowKnowing about Jesus will not make you sweet. Tasting Him will. Growth is what happens on the way down — from your head into your heart, and out through your hands.

Our heart for you

Sweet fruit comes
from a tree that has
tasted the water

You were made to bear fruit. And it is supposed to be sweet, not sour.

Here is the thing most of us get backwards. We try to fix the fruit. We work harder, behave better, sign up for more. But a fruit tree does not bear fruit because it knows about water. It bears fruit because it is planted by it.

Jeremiah describes a sick tree, and it is not a bad species — it is a fine tree in the wrong place. Wrong conditions, not wrong tree. That is a lot of us: right beliefs, mostly fruitless life. And that is not a verdict. It is a diagnosis with a cure.

You already know the difference. Nobody had to teach you what your favorite fruit tastes like when it is ripe — you know because you have tasted it. The Bible has a word for that kind of knowing: yada. Taste, not information. So the question underneath all of this is not whether you have roots. Everybody has roots. The question is what they are drinking.

Delight, not duty.

Why we say “grow in peace”

Most of what a
healthy tree grows,
nobody sees

A root system is about as wide as the branches above it. Nobody applauds roots. But roots are the reason the tree does not panic in a dry year.

01

Delight comes before fruit

Psalm 1 does not open with discipline. It opens with delight — and delight is the thing you return to without being told. Nobody reminds you to check the score of the game you care about. That is the kind of attention God is after, and it is on offer.

02

Roots reach

Jeremiah says the healthy tree sends its roots out toward the stream. It is not sitting beside the water; it is reaching for it — in your mornings, your marriage, your money, your calendar. That reaching is the whole practice.

03

Peace is what the roots buy you

The tree by the stream “will not worry in a year of drought.” That is what we mean by peace — not a calm season, but a root system deep enough to hold you when the season is not calm.

04

The fruit was never for the tree

We have never seen a tree eat its own fruit. What grows in you gets tasted by somebody else — at your kitchen table, in this room, in places you will never set foot. Anybody can count the seeds in a peach; nobody can count the peaches in a seed.

The path

Four steps
into the soil

01

1 · Show up on Sundays

Not once — give us five. One Sunday tells you whether you like the music. Five tell you whether these are your people, and that is the part that changes anything.

Plan a visit →
02

2 · Come to Discover SOJO

Last Sunday of the month, right after second service. We feed you a good lunch, tell you who we are, and — this is the part we care about most — we ask who you are. There is a shirt in your size waiting.

Pull up a chair →
03

3 · Take Discover More

A little teaching, a lot of discovering you — your story, your gifts, your heart — and where all of that fits in the life of this church.

All about Discover More →
04

4 · Get in a group

This is where planted happens. A table, a rhythm, and people with permission to ask you the hard question. Growth is impossible in a crowd and nearly inevitable at a table.

Find your group →

None of it is the point by itself. Every one of these is just a way of getting your roots closer to the water — and then it overflows, into serving and giving and going in purpose.

An invitation, not an indictment

Come get wet

If you have believed all the right things and never really been planted — that is not a failing grade. There is more available than what most of us are tasting, and it starts with one step, not four.
